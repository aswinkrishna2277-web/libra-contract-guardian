# Outreach Campaign — Target List & Playbook

**Goal:** 15 personalised messages to named people, sent before end of June 2026.
**Realistic yield:** 3–5 replies, 2–3 conversations, 1–2 that lead somewhere.
**The ask:** a short conversation about the research — NOT "do you have a job."

---

## Why the soft ask

A cold "are you hiring" puts the reader in a yes/no gate, and the honest answer is usually "not right now," which ends it. A request to talk about the work you're doing — from someone with a forthcoming EIPR paper and a working prototype — is interesting enough that a senior IP person will often say yes out of professional curiosity. The job conversation happens later, once they've met you and seen the work. Patience converts better than directness here.

## The personalisation rule

Every message needs ONE specific, true sentence showing you know who they are. Without it, the message is mail-merge and gets ignored. The sentence comes from:

- A recent article they wrote (best — you can respond to its substance)
- A talk or panel they were on
- A case they worked on that's public
- A position their firm took on the Government Report or a recent AI case

If you cannot find one true specific thing about a person, do not write to them — pick someone else. Fifteen well-aimed messages beat fifty generic ones.

---

## Sending mechanics

- **Channel:** email if you can find it (firm sites often list, or format is usually firstname.lastname@firm.com), otherwise LinkedIn. Email gets read more reliably for this audience.
- **Attach:** the one-page Evidence_Pack.pdf. Always.
- **Subject:** specific, not salesy. "Forthcoming EIPR article on AI-copyright disclosure procedure" works.
- **Length:** short. Four short paragraphs maximum. They're busy.
- **Timing:** Tuesday–Thursday, 8–10am UK time. Avoid Monday and Friday.
- **Follow-up:** if no reply in 10 days, ONE short follow-up ("just bumping this up your inbox in case it's of interest"). Never a third.
- **Tracking:** keep a simple spreadsheet — name, firm, date sent, date followed up, reply. You'll lose track otherwise across 15 people.

---

## The order to do it in

1. Start with QUB (your own institution — lowest-risk, and the scholarship conversation matters).
2. Then the 2–3 IP-boutique authors whose articles you can respond to specifically.
3. Then the legal-AI companies.
4. Then the rest.

Send in batches of 3–4 over a week, not all 15 at once. You'll learn from the early replies what framing lands and can adjust.

---

## What NOT to do

- Don't send the same message to all 15. The personalisation sentence must change every time.
- Don't attach the code or offer to send it. The evidence pack and an offer to demo on a call is the right level.
- Don't claim the paper is "published." It's "forthcoming" until it's in an issue.
- Don't oversell. Let the EIPR acceptance and the working prototype speak. Your earlier instinct — no arrogance — is exactly right.
- Don't chase reach. One accurate recipient beats a hundred impressions.
