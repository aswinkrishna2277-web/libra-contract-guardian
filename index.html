AI · Copyright · Civil Procedure

A procedure for proving what a model was trained on.

UK AI-copyright claims face a structural problem: the rights-holder cannot see the training data, and the developer is not obliged to show it. My work proposes a way through that, within the rules that already exist — and a working prototype that puts it into practice.

Forthcoming publicationEuropean Intellectual Property Review (Thomson Reuters)
Incoming, September 2026LLM in IP Law, Queen's University Belfast

The research

The Post-Report Provenance Procedure is a civil-procedure framework for managing the evidentiary asymmetry in UK AI-copyright litigation — requiring no new legislation, only the disciplined use of the Civil Procedure Rules as they stand.

  • i
    Prima facie trigger
    Establishing a good arguable case under CPR r.6.37 from hosted-repository, regurgitation, and statistical-attack evidence.
  • ii
    Model C disclosure
    The feasibility of request-led Extended Disclosure under Practice Direction 57AD — including the IPEC carve-out at paragraph 1.4 that most analyses overlook.
  • iii
    Adverse inference
    The availability of an inference at trial under the Wisniewski line, as refined by Cockerill J in Magdeev v Tsvetkov.

The software

Alongside the paper I have built its operational form: software that takes a UK AI-copyright fact pattern, maps it to the three stages, and produces a structured procedural assessment. Three choices set it apart from general-purpose legal AI.

  • Verified, not generated Every authority is drawn from a database cross-checked against BAILII, legislation.gov.uk, EUR-Lex and CURIA. The model cannot invent a citation; a verifier rejects any that does not trace to a real source.
  • Deterministic scoring The procedural assessment is computed in code from structured signals. The model only phrases a result that has already been determined and checked.
  • Local by design It runs entirely on the user's own machine. No cloud, no third-party API, no client data leaving the device — deployable on matters where confidentiality is the gating concern.

Status

The framework is accepted for publication, forthcoming in the European Intellectual Property Review. The software is a working prototype — a demonstration of the research in operational form, not a commercial product. I am glad to walk through it on a call.
